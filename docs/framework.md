# Niche decision framework

Choose a niche based on **demonstrated audience demand**, **realistic earning
opportunities**, and **your ability to produce original content consistently**.
A high advertised CPM alone is insufficient.

This is a practical decision aid — not a scientifically validated scoring
system or a guarantee of earnings.

| # | Criterion | What to evaluate | Evidence to look for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Audience demand | Is there sustained interest in this subject? | Recent videos attracting views across several channels, recurring audience questions, search interest over time. |
| 2 | Opportunity for a new channel | Can you identify a specific audience or need that existing videos serve poorly? | Smaller channels gaining traction repeatedly; unanswered questions; gaps in clarity, language, demonstrations or coverage. |
| 3 | Revenue opportunities | What could generate income besides advertising? | Relevant affiliate programs, sponsors already active in the niche, fitting products or services. Their existence does not establish your likely earnings. |
| 4 | Audience specificity | Can you clearly describe who the channel serves and why they watch? | A defined audience, language, recurring problem or entertainment interest. "Technology" is broad; "beginner computer troubleshooting" is specific. |
| 5 ★ | Original contribution | What useful or entertaining value can you add? | Your own demonstrations, research, explanations, comparisons, interviews or storytelling. |
| 6 | Content depth | Can the niche support many substantially different videos? | A topic map with multiple series and follow-up questions — not repeated versions of one idea. |
| 7 | Return-viewing potential | Does one video naturally create interest in another? | Connected lessons, recurring formats, character-driven stories, related problems for the same audience. |
| 8 ★ | Production economics | Can you afford the time and money to maintain quality? | Measured research, scripting, narration, editing, licensing and revision costs from pilot videos. |
| 9 | Knowledge and credibility | Can you explain the subject accurately and verify claims? | Relevant experience, dependable sources, expert access, a workable fact-checking process. |
| 10 ★ | Monetization and rights suitability | Can the content comply with YouTube policies and use licensed material? | Original scripts and visuals, documented permissions, advertiser-suitable content. |

★ = minimum requirement (gate). In this repo a gate passes at a score of 3/5 or more.

## Rules the tool enforces

- **Gates first.** Policy compliance, originality and affordable production are
  minimum requirements. A niche failing any gate is not ranked.
- **Then demand and reason-to-choose.** Among eligible niches, favour the
  strongest evidence of demand and the clearest reason for viewers to choose
  your channel (mean of criteria 1, 2 and 5), then the overall mean.
- **Evidence, not opinion.** Any score of 3 or more needs at least one evidence
  entry (URL or saved scan). Opinion alone can only score ≤ 2.
- **One viral video is not demand.** Scans flag when one channel holds most of
  the views, when breakouts dwarf the median, and when uploads don't span
  enough months. YouTube identifies topic interest, competition and seasonality
  as factors affecting reach.
- **RPM, not CPM.** CPM is advertiser spend per 1,000 ad impressions before
  YouTube's share. RPM is creator revenue after revenue sharing per 1,000 views
  (Shorts: engaged views) and can include several YouTube revenue sources.
- **Profit formula.** Estimated monthly operating profit = YouTube revenue +
  external revenue − operating costs. Before you have revenue data, use
  labelled scenarios. A competitor's views do not reveal their revenue or profit.
- **Originality for faceless, AI-assisted channels.** AI-assisted content can
  be monetized, but repetitive or mass-produced content can be ineligible.
  Each niche needs 20 distinct ideas; near-duplicates are flagged.

## Decision process

1. Shortlist three niches; define the audience and channel promise for each.
2. Review recent performance across several channels, including smaller ones.
   Compare videos of similar age and format; look at typical results alongside
   breakouts.
3. Draft 20 distinct video ideas per niche (a planning exercise, not a YouTube
   requirement).
4. Identify specific revenue routes and calculate production costs.
5. Produce a small pilot batch for the strongest candidate. Evaluate viewing
   behaviour, audience responses, production effort and commercial interest.
